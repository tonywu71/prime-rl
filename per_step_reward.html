Per-step rewards: two ways to weight a token's contribution

GRPO per-turn self-judge · 2026-06-01

Two approaches, one underlying mechanism. Policy-gradient RL weights each token by an advantage value — that multiplier is the answer to "weight a token's contribution to the update." The two approaches differ only in where those values come from.

Today prime-rl broadcasts one scalar to every token (Approach A). Per-turn self-judge (B) has the policy grade its own progress every turn — those grades reshape the scalar advantage multiplicatively while preserving total gradient mass.

Variable reference (colors match equations below)

  • rreward — scalar signal. $\rew_t$ is the reward at step $t$; today prime-rl emits one $\rew$ per rollout.
  • Aadvantage — the per-token weight multiplied into the gradient. $\adv_t$ is the weight on step $t$'s tokens.
  • Greturn / reward-to-go — discounted sum of step $t$'s reward and all future rewards: $\ret_t = \sum_{t' \ge t} \disc^{t'-t} \rew_{t'}$.
  • Vbaseline / value — reference subtracted from $\ret_t$ to reduce variance. GRPO uses the group mean.
  • γdiscount — $0 \le \disc \le 1$; how much future rewards decay.
  • πpolicy — the model $\pol_\theta$; $\pol(a_t | s_t)$ is the probability of token $a_t$.
  • vself-judge label value — $\vt_t \in \{-1, 0, +1, +2\}$ for REGRESS/NEUTRAL/PROGRESS/ACHIEVED.
  • wraw per-turn weight — sign-aware scaling factor before normalization.
  • mnormalized multiplier — $\mt_t$ after token-mass renormalization.
  • αself-judge strength — $\al = 0$ collapses to vanilla GRPO; $\al = 1$ is full weighting.

01The advantage is the per-token weight

The policy-gradient theorem is a sum over steps, where each step's log-prob gradient is multiplied by a scalar weight:

$$\nabla J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\;\sum_t \underbrace{\adv_t}_{\text{weight}} \cdot \nabla \log \pol_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)\;\right], \qquad \adv_t = \ret_t - \val(s_t), \qquad \ret_t = \sum_{t' \ge t} \disc^{\,t'-t}\,\rew_{t'}$$

$\adv_t$ is exactly the "multiply the token's contribution" mechanism the question asks about. Two things the formula immediately settles:

  • Multiply, not bias. $\adv_t$ scales $\nabla\log\pol$. An additive bias on the loss has no per-token gradient (constants differentiate to zero); biasing logits reshapes the optimal policy. Multiplicative weighting is the unbiased, principled form — and it is what's already wired up in loss.py:151.
  • Future credit, not immediate reward. The return $\ret_t$ contains $\rew_t, \rew_{t+1}, \ldots$ but not past rewards. A reward at step 5 should credit steps 1–5, not steps 6–10. Weighting a token by the bare immediate $\rew_t$ is wrong — the correct weight is the return-to-go minus a baseline.

The explicit loss function

Autograd can't implement $\nabla J$ directly — it needs a scalar loss whose gradient is $-\nabla J$. The surrogate is the advantage-weighted importance ratio. Two notation notes: the theorem's step index $t$ becomes a token index $k$ (in LLM RL every generated token is one action), and the loss is calligraphic $\mathcal{L}$ — the plain $L$ used later in this doc is the token count.

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta) \;=\; -\sum_{k} \adv_k \,\rho_k(\theta), \qquad \rho_k(\theta) \;=\; \frac{\pol_\theta(a_k \mid s_k)}{\pol_{\text{inf}}(a_k \mid s_k)}$$

$\adv_k$ is data — computed in the orchestrator, no gradient flows through it. Differentiating recovers the policy gradient, because $\nabla \rho_k = \rho_k \nabla\log\pol_\theta(a_k \mid s_k)$:

$$-\nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta) \;=\; \sum_k \adv_k\,\rho_k(\theta)\,\nabla\log\pol_\theta(a_k \mid s_k) \;\;\xrightarrow{\;\rho_k \,\to\, 1\;}\;\; \sum_k \adv_k\,\nabla\log\pol_\theta(a_k \mid s_k)$$

On-policy ($\rho_k = 1$) this is exactly the theorem above; off-policy, the ratio reweights each token by how far the trainer has drifted from the sampling policy. prime-rl's full loss (default_loss_fn, loss.py:150–153) is this surrogate plus a trust-region keep-mask and a quadratic KL penalty:

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta) \;=\; -\,\tau_{\text{adv}} \sum_{k \,\in\, \text{keep}} \adv_k\, \rho_k(\theta) \;+\; \tau_{\text{KL}} \sum_{k} \big(\log \rho_k(\theta)\big)^2$$

$\text{keep}$ = trainable tokens that pass the DPPO trust-region mask (keep_mask); $\tau_{\text{adv}}$ = adv_tau, $\tau_{\text{KL}}$ = kl_tau. The mask and KL terms apply identically under every approach below, so the rest of this doc works with the bare surrogate $-\sum_k \adv_k \rho_k$.

"Quadratic KL" is shorthand — $(\log\rho_k)^2$ is not literally a KL divergence. It is the k2 estimator (Schulman): a per-sample quantity whose expectation matches the true KL to second order. Write $\delta_k = \log\rho_k$ and perturb $\pol_\theta = \pol_{\text{inf}}(1+\varepsilon)$; since $\mathbb{E}_{\pol_{\text{inf}}}[\varepsilon] = 0$ (both policies normalize), Taylor expansion gives

$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pol_{\text{inf}} \,\|\, \pol_\theta) = \mathbb{E}[-\delta] \approx \tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon^2] \approx \mathbb{E}\big[\tfrac{1}{2}\delta^2\big]$

— identical to second order, exact as the policies converge (the regime a trust-region penalty cares about). The quadratic form is preferred as a loss over the unbiased estimator $-\delta_k$ because it is per-sample non-negative and its gradient $2\log\rho_k \,\nabla\log\pol_\theta$ acts like a spring in log-prob space — restoring force proportional to the drift, zero at zero drift. The code also computes a second estimator, $\rho - \log\rho - 1$ (k3, mismatch_kl, loss.py:112) — equally second-order-exact, but used only as a monitoring metric, never trained.

In prime-rl the per-token loss already uses a [seq]-shaped advantage tensor element-wise:

Complete worked example: rewards → returns → advantage

Three steps turn raw rewards into the per-token weight. Every number here flows directly into Approach A (§04) and Approach B (§05).

This is why GRPO broadcasts. The flat $\adv_t$ in the last column is exactly the scalar §02 copies onto every token. Rollout #1's +0.35 and #2's −0.40 are the precise inputs the self-judge reshapes in §05. The only way to get a non-flat $\adv_t$ within a rollout under prime-rl is redistribution of the scalar (Approach B).

Wait — $\adv_1 = \adv_2 = \cdots = \adv_T$? Is that even correct? Yes — it is the exact policy-gradient advantage, not an approximation. But the flatness is not a general truth; it is the consequence of three assumptions holding at once:

  • $\disc = 1$ — no decay, so future reward reaches every earlier turn undamped;
  • terminal-only reward — one scalar $\rew$ per rollout, so $\ret_t = \rew$ for every $t$ (the partial sums $\sum_{t' \ge t}$ all contain the same single term);
  • constant baseline — GRPO's $\val$ is the group mean, the same number at every step, not a state-dependent $V(s_t)$.

Together: $\adv_t = \ret_t - \val = \rew - \bar{\rew}$ — the same value at every $t$. Break any assumption and the flatness disappears: with $\disc < 1$ the returns decay per step (Step 1: $0.656 \to 1.000$); with intermediate rewards the returns-to-go differ; with a learned critic $V(s_t)$ the baseline differs per state. prime-rl satisfies all three by construction, so the broadcast is exactly right in the unbiased-gradient sense. It is not credit-precise, though: a state-dependent baseline would differentiate good turns from bad ones within the rollout — which is what Approach B approximates without a critic.

How the loss simplifies under flat advantages. Substitute $\adv_k \equiv \adv$ into the surrogate from above — the constant factors out of the token sum:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{GRPO}}(\theta) \;=\; -\sum_{k=1}^{L} \adv\,\rho_k(\theta) \;=\; -\,\adv \sum_{k=1}^{L} \rho_k(\theta)$$

The rollout's entire loss collapses to one scalar times the sum of its $L$ token importance-ratios. For rollout #1 above: $\mathcal{L} = -0.35 \sum_{k=1}^{200} \rho_k$. Every token's gradient carries the identical multiplier $\adv$ — this is "broadcast" written as a loss. Two immediate consequences: rollout #4 ($\adv = 0$) contributes exactly zero loss and zero gradient from all of its tokens, and within any rollout the optimizer has no way to push turn 5 harder than turn 2 — the temporal structure §04 complains about is gone before the gradient is ever computed.

prime-rl has no $\disc$ knob. The advantage code (orchestrator/advantage.py:70) is pure GRPO over a single scalar reward per rollout — no return-to-go, no discount factor anywhere in the tree. prime-rl is the $\disc=1$, terminal-only case above; with exactly one reward per rollout, discounting is moot regardless of $\disc$. The $\disc$ in Step 1 is general policy-gradient theory, not a parameter you can set today.

04Approach A: Vanilla GRPO

One scalar advantage per rollout, computed by GRPO group-normalization:

$$\adv_i = \rew_i - \underbrace{\frac{1}{K}\sum_{j=1}^{K} \rew_j}_{\text{group mean (baseline)}} \qquad K = \text{rollouts in group}$$

src/prime_rl/orchestrator/advantage.py:70 — rewards - rewards.mean()

That scalar is then broadcast to every assistant token. All turns in a trajectory receive identical gradient scaling. No temporal structure survives.

The stall mode. If all $K$ rollouts on a hard task fail: rewards $= [0,\ldots,0]$, group mean $= 0$, advantages $= [0,\ldots,0]$, gradient $= 0$. The model learns nothing that step. This is the primary motivation for Approach B.

05Approach B: Per-turn self-judge

Instead of an external verifier, the policy grades its own action — after the environment has responded, so the action's outcome is visible. Each grade is a discrete progress label, and the label sequence reshapes the scalar GRPO advantage multiplicatively — no new reward signal, only redistribution.

This is implemented in-tree. The wrapper env environments/self_judge_wrapper/self_judge_wrapper.py emits the per-turn labels into state["_progress_labels"]; the orchestrator reshapes advantages in src/prime_rl/orchestrator/self_judge.py (attach_self_judge_advantages_to_rollout, line 74). It is enabled per-run by an [orchestrator.self_judge] config block (SelfJudgeConfig, packages/prime-rl-configs/.../configs/orchestrator.py:445); omit the block for plain scalar GRPO.

Key properties of this design:

  • Label is a pure side-channel. The judge completion is never recorded as a trajectory step and never receives gradient, so there are no format constraints and no parsing fragility — it only ever reshapes the action tokens' advantages.
  • Outcome-grounded. Because the judge fires from env_response (after the action is executed), it grades $a_t$ with its result $o_{t+1}$ in context — not by guessing from the action text alone.
  • Prefix reuse. The judge prompt extends the same conversation prefix that produced $a_t$, so the expensive attention over history is cached; the judge adds only the short observation+question and its one-word answer.
  • No format engineering needed. Earlier in-band approaches required a mandatory PLAN: line placed outside <think> with 95–100% adherence monitoring. A separate judge completion eliminates all of that.

The weighting math

src/prime_rl/orchestrator/self_judge.py:159 — _compute_multipliers (Steps 1–2); per-token write-back at self_judge.py:127 (Step 3)

Step 1 — raw per-turn weight. Sign-aware: in a successful rollout ($\adv > 0$), good labels amplify; in a failed one ($\adv < 0$), bad labels take more blame.

$$\wt_t \;=\; \max\!\big(\varepsilon,\; 1 \;+\; \al \cdot \vt_t \cdot \operatorname{sign}(\adv)\big), \qquad \varepsilon = 0.05$$

The floor $\varepsilon$ (WEIGHT_FLOOR, self_judge.py:50) keeps every weight positive so a single adverse label can't flip its own turn's advantage sign — a no-op at $\al \le 0.5$, but a guard at higher $\al$.

Step 2 — token-mass renormalization. Redistribution only — the total advantage mass is conserved regardless of labels.

$$\mt_t \;=\; \wt_t \;\cdot\; \frac{L}{\displaystyle\sum_s \wt_s \,\len_s} \qquad L = \sum_s \len_s = \text{total tokens}, \quad \len_s = \text{tokens in step }s$$

Step 3 — per-token advantage.

$$A^{\text{tok}}_t \;=\; \adv \;\cdot\; \mt_t$$

Verification (mass conservation): $\sum_t A^{\text{tok}}_t \cdot \len_t = \adv \cdot \sum_t \mt_t \len_t = \adv \cdot L$. ✓ Identical to scalar GRPO when all labels are uniform ($\wt_t = 1$ for all $t$).

The full new loss. Substitute $A^{\text{tok}}_t$ into the §01 surrogate. Each token $k$ belongs to exactly one turn $t(k)$, so the token sum factors into a sum over the $T$ turns:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{SJ}}(\theta) \;=\; -\sum_{k=1}^{L} \adv\;\mt_{t(k)}\;\rho_k(\theta) \;=\; -\,\adv \sum_{t=1}^{T} \mt_t \underbrace{\sum_{k \,\in\, \text{turn } t} \rho_k(\theta)}_{\text{turn }t\text{'s ratio mass}}, \qquad \mt_t \;=\; \frac{\wt_t\, L}{\sum_s \wt_s \len_s},\quad \wt_t = \max\!\big(\varepsilon,\, 1 + \al\,\vt_t \operatorname{sign}(\adv)\big)$$

Compare with $\mathcal{L}_{\text{GRPO}} = -\adv\sum_k \rho_k$ from §01: the only change is the per-turn factor $\mt_t$ inserted between the scalar and each turn's ratio mass. The trust-region keep-mask and KL penalty apply on top, unchanged. Two sanity checks fall straight out of the equation:

  • $\al = 0$ recovers GRPO exactly. Every $\wt_t = 1 \Rightarrow \mt_t = 1$, and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{SJ}} = -\adv\sum_k \rho_k = \mathcal{L}_{\text{GRPO}}$ — the self-judge is a strict generalization, not a different objective.
  • On-policy the loss value is unchanged — only its gradient distribution shifts. At the first gradient step $\rho_k = 1$, so each turn's ratio mass is $\len_t$ and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{SJ}} = -\adv\sum_t \mt_t \len_t = -\adv L = \mathcal{L}_{\text{GRPO}}$. This is mass conservation written as a loss: labels never change how much total signal the rollout contributes, only which turns' log-probs carry it.

Why labels can never change the total. Substitute $\mt_t$ into the token-weighted sum: the renormalizer $\sum_s \wt_s \len_s$ is exactly the denominator, so it cancels — leaving $L$ no matter what the $\wt_t$ (hence the labels) are:

$$\sum_t \mt_t \len_t \;=\; \frac{L}{\displaystyle\sum_s \wt_s \len_s}\;\sum_t \wt_t \len_t \;=\; L \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \sum_t A^{\text{tok}}_t \len_t \;=\; \adv \sum_t \mt_t \len_t \;=\; \adv\,L$$

Concretely, three different label assignments for the 5-turn example ($\len_s = 40$, $L = 200$, $\al = 0.5$, sign$(\adv) = +1$). The per-turn normalizer $\sum_s \wt_s \len_s$ differs every time, yet the conserved mass $\sum_t \mt_t \len_t$ is always $L$:

The "all PROGRESS" row is the inflation attack: every $\mt_t = 1.0$, identical to vanilla GRPO. Labelling every turn PROGRESS redistributes nothing — it buys the policy nothing. Labels only ever move mass between turns; they can never add or remove it.

Anti-hack design

Why the policy wants to inflate labels. Look at the gradient update for a single token in turn $t$ of a successful rollout ($\adv > 0$):

$$\Delta\theta \;\propto\; \underbrace{\adv \cdot \mt_t}_{A^{\text{tok}}_t} \cdot \nabla\log\pol_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)$$

A larger $\mt_t$ means a stronger push to repeat those tokens. In the success example: labelling turn 3 PROGRESS ($\mt = 1.154$) vs. NEUTRAL ($\mt = 0.769$) gives its tokens 50% more gradient reinforcement. The policy is its own judge — and it benefits from judging itself generously. The self-serving optimum is to label every turn PROGRESS or ACHIEVED, claiming maximum credit everywhere.

In failed rollouts the incentive flips. With $\adv < 0$, a larger $\mt_t$ means more blame. The policy minimises blame by labelling turns ACHIEVED ($\wt = 0$, so $\mt = 0$ — escaping all blame) or PROGRESS ($\wt = 0.5$, half the blame of NEUTRAL). Both are modes of mislabelling to dodge the gradient's punishment.

These two failure modes need different mitigations. Four levers address them — the first two are always on, the last two are config flags on SelfJudgeConfig (both default true):

07When to use which approach

Start with A (GRPO) unless you have intra-group variance collapse. GRPO is simple, stable, and the right default. Move to B only when you can observe the stall mode: all-zero or near-zero advantages across a group, no learning.
Use B (self-judge) when: Dense scalar reward unblocks training (variance relief works) but you need further discrimination within a rollout — e.g. long multi-turn episodes where one bad action at step 2 of 30 drags down 30 good actions. Watch for anti-hack violation rates in the metrics.
Two different bottlenecks — don't confuse them.
  • Group collapse (variance, across rollouts). GRPO has no critic; the advantage is purely relative: $A_i = \rew_i - \bar{\rew}_{\text{group}}$. If every rollout in a group scores the same (all fail → all $0$, or all succeed → all $1$), the mean equals each reward, every advantage is $0$, and the gradient vanishes. The signal comes entirely from intra-group spread — zero spread, zero learning.
  • Credit misassignment (within a rollout). One bad action at turn 2 drags down 30 good actions because they all share one scalar — the problem B addresses.

A principled test before moving from A to B: ship a scalar milestone-fraction ($M/N$) reward first. A denser scalar spreads a collapsed group's rewards across a range → non-zero variance → gradient flows again — without any within-rollout credit. So if that alone unblocks training, the bottleneck was group collapse, not credit assignment, and B adds nothing. Only if a dense scalar doesn't unblock it — yet you can point to specific trajectories where one late mistake punishes earlier good steps — is per-step redistribution (B) solving a real problem.
